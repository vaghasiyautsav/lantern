# Kept Flame

*Design philosophy for the Lantern mark*

**Kept Flame** is the aesthetics of a light that is owned, not rented. Its
entire visual argument is a single warm source held inside a quiet, dark
field — warmth that radiates outward to the edge of its own small world and
deliberately no further. The composition is built on containment as a
virtue: the glow does not bleed off the canvas, because the canvas *is* the
network — everything the light touches is local, known, and near. What lies
beyond the boundary is simply not addressed. This restraint must read as
confidence, and confidence at this scale is the product of painstaking,
master-level control of gradients, radii, and edge tension.

Form is reduced until nothing optional remains. One vessel, one flame, one
field. The vessel is drawn with the fewest possible strokes — a silhouette
a child could trace and a typographer would respect — because the mark must
survive brutal miniaturization without losing its soul. Every curve is a
negotiated settlement between geometry and warmth: mathematically clean
superellipses softened by optical corrections that only someone at the top
of their craft would think to make. Nothing decorative, nothing literal,
no texture for texture's sake. If a detail does not survive at sixteen
pixels, it does not deserve to exist at a thousand.

Color is a two-voice chord. The field: deep, blue-black indigo — night on a
quiet street, screens off, the hum of a local network. The source: amber
into gold — tungsten, not neon; a kept flame, not an alarm. Between them a
narrow bridge of desaturated blue light where the glow meets the dark, tuned
with the patience of a colorist who has rebalanced it a hundred times. No
third hue is permitted. The chord must feel inevitable, as if these two
temperatures had always belonged to each other.

Space is arranged as a still ceremony. The source sits at the exact optical
center — fractionally above geometric center, the correction every master
knows — and the dark field breathes around it with generous, deliberate
margin. Radiance is expressed not by rays but by graduated luminance:
patient concentric falloff, each step imperceptible, the accumulated labor
of countless refinements. Any suggestion of connection — of others warmed
by the same light — is made with the faintest possible geometry, felt
before it is seen.

The finished mark must look inevitable: as if it were discovered rather
than drawn, then polished for weeks. It should sit beside the great app
marks without apology — crisp at every scale, luminous without glare,
warm without kitsch. This is the discipline of Kept Flame: one light,
kept close, made perfect.
